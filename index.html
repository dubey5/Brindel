Small on purpose

Brindel does one job. It remembers what you copied and gets out of the way.

Your last five

The picker shows your five most recent copies, newest first. Copy the same thing again and it moves to the top instead of repeating.

Text and images

Copy a paragraph or a screenshot. Both appear in the list, and images get a small thumbnail so you can tell them apart.

Pin one thing

Keep the item you paste all day, like an address or a reply, at the top until you unpin it.

Instant paste

Press Ctrl+Cmd+V to paste your pinned item, or your latest copy, straight into the app you are in. Nothing opens.

Opens at your cursor

The picker appears where you are working. Click anywhere else or press Esc and it disappears without pasting.

Stays on your Mac

Your history is stored on your Mac. There is no account, and Brindel does not send your clipboard anywhere.

Keyboard first

Brindel lives in your menu bar, with no Dock icon. You can set it to launch at login. Everything else is a key press.

  • Open the picker at your cursor
    ⌘⇧V
  • Paste the pinned or latest item instantly
    ⌃⌘V
  • Move through the list
    ↑↓
  • Paste the selected item
    Enter
  • Paste a row directly
    1–5
  • Pin or unpin the selected item
    P
  • Close without pasting
    Esc

Installing takes two minutes

Brindel is an early preview and is not yet notarised by Apple, so macOS asks you to confirm the first launch. You only do this once. No Terminal needed.

  1. Open the download

    Double-click the Brindel disk image (.dmg) in your Downloads folder.

  2. Move it to Applications

    Drag the Brindel icon onto the Applications folder in the window that appears.

  3. Open Brindel

    Launch it from Applications. macOS says it cannot verify the app. Choose Done.

  4. Approve the first launch

    Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security. Scroll to Security, choose Open Anyway beside Brindel, and confirm.

  5. Allow the two permissions

    Choose Get started on the welcome card. macOS asks to let Brindel control System Events, then for Accessibility. Allow both.

  6. Restart Brindel once

    Quit Brindel from its menu bar icon and open it again. Then press ⌘ ⇧ V to try it.

During the preview, each new version may ask you to switch Accessibility on again. This ends once Brindel is notarised.

Questions

Why does macOS warn me before opening Brindel?

Apple shows that warning for any app that has not been notarised through its paid developer programme. Brindel is in early preview and has not been through that yet. The steps above open it safely, and the warning goes away once Brindel is notarised.

Why does Brindel need Accessibility access?

To paste for you. After you choose an item, Brindel sends a Command+V key press to the app you were using. macOS only allows that when you have switched Accessibility on for the app.

Why does it ask about System Events?

Brindel asks System Events which app is in front when you open the picker, so your paste lands where you were working. It does not read anything else.

Is my clipboard sent anywhere?

No. Your history is stored on your Mac. Brindel has no account and does not send your clipboard anywhere.

Pasting does not work. What now?

Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Accessibility, and check that Brindel is on. If it is on but still fails, select it, press the minus button to remove it, then quit and reopen Brindel and allow it again. This is most common right after an update.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 10.15 Catalina or later, on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs.

Is there a Windows or iPhone version?

Windows is planned for later. An iPhone version is not planned at this time.
